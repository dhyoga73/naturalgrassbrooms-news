What is a nipa palm broom?

The nipa palm (Nypa fruticans) is a palm that grows along coasts, estuaries and mangrove areas in Southeast Asia, including Indonesia. Brooms made from nipa palm material are known locally as sapu nypah, and in export catalogues you will also see them spelled nypa palm broom, nypah broom or simply nipa broom. Different spellings, same product family.

Like other natural brooms, it is made from plant material rather than plastic, which makes it a fit for retailers and distributors who want natural or plastic-free cleaning products in their range.

How to choose the right size for your customers

There is no single "best" length. The right choice depends on who will use the broom and where it will be sold. A few practical questions help:

  • Who is the end user? Shorter brooms are easier to handle and store in small homes and apartments. Longer brooms let a standing user sweep with less bending.
  • Indoor or outdoor? For balconies, terraces, courtyards and larger areas, buyers often prefer a longer, heavier broom that covers more ground per stroke. For quick indoor jobs, a lighter broom is often enough.
  • How will it be displayed? Check shelf height and display racks in your retail channel. A 120 cm broom may need a different display solution than an 80 cm one.
  • What price point are you aiming for? A larger, heavier broom uses more material, so expect it to sit at a different price level. Ask for a quotation per size rather than assuming.

If you are new to the product, a sample of each length is the easiest way to compare feel, weight and finish before you commit to a size mix.

How carton packing affects your landed cost

Pieces per carton matters as much as the price per broom. The 80 cm model packs 100 pieces per carton, while the 120 cm model packs 40. That means the same number of cartons gives you very different quantities of brooms, and freight is usually charged on volume or weight, not on pieces.

To compare sizes fairly, work out the freight cost per broom, not per carton:

  1. Get the carton dimensions and gross weight for each size.
  2. Estimate how many cartons fit your shipment (for example, a share of a container or a consolidated shipment).
  3. Divide the freight and other import costs by the number of brooms, then add that to the product price.

Many importers end up mixing sizes in one order: a high-volume size for everyday retail plus a smaller quantity of a longer model for customers who want it. If you plan a mixed order, list the quantity per size in your RFQ so the supplier can confirm the packing plan.

FAQ

Is a nypa broom the same as a nipa broom?

Yes. Nipa, nypa and nypah are different spellings of the same palm; sapu nypah is the Indonesian name.

Are the weights exact?

No. The weights are approximate because each broom is handmade from natural material. Ask for confirmed specifications with your quotation.

Can I order more than one size in the same order?

You can ask for any combination of the 80, 105 and 120 cm models in one enquiry. Include the quantity per size in your RFQ.

Which size should I start with?

It depends on your end users and retail channel. If you are unsure, request samples of each length and compare them with what already sells in your market.
